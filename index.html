Escape Speed (Escape Velocity)

Escape speed on earth (or any other planet) is defined as the minimum speed with which a body has to be projected vertically upwards from the surface of earth so that it just crosses the gravitational field of earth and never returns on its own.

First Method (Calculus)

Work done in taking a body of mass \(m\) against gravitational attraction from \(x\) to \(x + dx\):

$$ dW = F \, dx = \frac{GMm}{x^2} dx $$

Total work from surface (\(x=R\)) to infinity (\(x=\infty\)):

$$ W = \int_{R}^{\infty} \frac{GMm}{x^2} dx = GMm \left[ \frac{-1}{x} \right]_{R}^{\infty} = \frac{GMm}{R} $$

This work is provided by initial Kinetic Energy:

$$ \frac{1}{2}mv_e^2 = \frac{GMm}{R} \implies v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} $$

Second Method (Conservation of Energy)

Let \(v_e\) be the escape speed. At the surface of earth, Total Energy = K.E. + P.E.

At infinity, Total Energy is zero (barely escapes).

$$ \frac{1}{2}mv_e^2 + \left(-\frac{GMm}{R}\right) = 0 $$ $$ \implies \frac{1}{2}mv_e^2 = \frac{GMm}{R} \implies v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} $$

Since \( g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \), we can also write \( v_e = \sqrt{2gR} \). For Earth, \( v_e \approx 11.2 \text{ km/s} \).

Satellites

A satellite is a body which is revolving continuously in an orbit around a comparatively much larger body.

1. Geostationary (Geosynchronous) Satellites

  • Appears fixed at a definite height to an observer on Earth.
  • Time period: 24 hours (synchronous with Earth's rotation).
  • Height: Approx. 36,000 km above the equator.
  • Orbital Speed: Approx. 3.1 km/s.
  • Direction: West to East (same as Earth).
  • Uses: Communication (e.g., INSAT series, Telstar).

2. Polar Satellites

  • Revolve in polar orbits (inclination of \(90^\circ\) with equatorial plane).
  • Altitude: Low altitude, typically 500 to 800 km.
  • Time period: Approx. 100 minutes.
  • Crosses any location on Earth many times a day.
  • Uses: Remote sensing, weather monitoring, environmental studies (e.g., IRS series).

Orbital Speed of a Satellite

Orbital speed (\(v_0\)) is the minimum speed required to put the satellite into a given orbit around earth.

Step-by-Step Derivation

Let \(M\) = mass of Earth, \(R\) = radius of Earth.

Let \(m\) = mass of satellite, \(v\) = orbital speed.

Let \(h\) = height of satellite, so orbit radius \(r = R + h\).


Centripetal force required to keep satellite in orbit:

$$ F_c = \frac{mv^2}{r} $$

This is provided by the gravitational pull:

$$ F_g = \frac{GMm}{r^2} $$

In equilibrium, \( F_c = F_g \):

$$ \frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{GMm}{r^2} $$ $$ v^2 = \frac{GM}{r} \implies v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R+h}} $$

Key Observations:

  • Orbital speed is independent of the mass (\(m\)) of the satellite.
  • It decreases with an increase in radius of orbit (\(r\)).
  • If the satellite orbits very close to Earth's surface (\(h \ll R\)), then \(r \approx R\).
$$ v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}} = \sqrt{gR} $$

For Earth, \(v_0 \approx 7.92 \text{ km/s} \)

Time Period of a Satellite

The time taken by a satellite to complete one revolution around the earth is its Time Period (\(T\)).

Distance travelled in one revolution = \(2\pi r\)

Orbital velocity = \(v\)

$$ T = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{velocity}} = \frac{2\pi r}{v} $$

Substituting \(v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}\):

$$ T = \frac{2\pi r}{\sqrt{GM/r}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}} $$ $$ T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{(R+h)^3}{GM}} $$

Special Case & Altitude

For a satellite orbiting very close to Earth (\(h \ll R\)):

$$ T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{R}{g}} \approx 84.6 \text{ minutes} $$

Squaring the formula gives altitude \(h\):

$$ h = \left( \frac{T^2 R^2 g}{4\pi^2} \right)^{1/3} - R $$

Angular Momentum (L)

When a satellite of mass \(m\) orbits with linear speed \(v\) in an orbit of radius \(r\), its angular momentum is:

$$ L = m v r $$ $$ L = m \left( \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} \right) r = m \sqrt{GMr} $$

Angular momentum depends on both the mass of the satellite (\(m\)), the mass of the planet (\(M\)), and the radius of the orbit (\(r\)).

Energy of an Orbiting Satellite

The total mechanical energy (\(E\)) of a satellite is the sum of its potential energy (\(U\)) and kinetic energy (\(K\)).

1. Potential Energy (U)

Due to position w.r.t Earth. It is negative indicating binding force.

$$ U = -\frac{GMm}{r} $$

2. Kinetic Energy (K)

Due to its orbital motion (\(v = \sqrt{GM/r}\)).

$$ K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{GMm}{2r} $$

3. Total Energy (E)

$$ E = U + K = -\frac{GMm}{r} + \frac{GMm}{2r} = -\frac{GMm}{2r} $$

The negative sign signifies that the satellite is bound to Earth.

Binding Energy

The energy required to remove the satellite from its orbit to infinity.

$$ \text{Binding Energy} = -E = +\frac{GMm}{2r} $$

Q1: The escape velocity from Earth is \(11.2 \text{ km/s}\). What will be the escape velocity from a planet whose mass and radius are twice that of Earth?

$$ v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} $$ For new planet: \( M' = 2M, R' = 2R \) $$ v_e' = \sqrt{\frac{2G(2M)}{2R}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = v_e $$

Answer: 11.2 km/s

Q2: An artificial satellite circles around the Earth at a distance of 3600 km from the surface. Find its orbital velocity. (Given: \(R = 6400 \text{ km}\), \(g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2\))

\( h = 3600 \text{ km}, R = 6400 \text{ km} \implies r = R+h = 10000 \text{ km} = 10^7 \text{ m} \) $$ v = R \sqrt{\frac{g}{r}} $$ $$ v = 6.4 \times 10^6 \sqrt{\frac{10}{10^7}} = 6.4 \times 10^6 \times 10^{-3} = 6.4 \times 10^3 \text{ m/s} $$

Answer: 6.4 km/s

Q3: Find the period of revolution of the satellite in Q2.

$$ T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} $$ $$ T = \frac{2 \times 3.14 \times 10^7}{6.4 \times 10^3} \approx 9812.5 \text{ s} $$

Answer: 9812.5 s (or ~2.72 hours)

Q4: A satellite of mass 500 kg orbits Earth at height \(3.6 \times 10^6 \text{ m}\). Calculate its kinetic energy. (\(M_e = 6 \times 10^{24} \text{ kg}\), \(R_e = 6.4 \times 10^6 \text{ m}\), \(G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N m}^2/\text{kg}^2\))

\( r = R_e + h = 6.4 \times 10^6 + 3.6 \times 10^6 = 10^7 \text{ m} \) $$ K = \frac{GMm}{2r} $$ $$ K = \frac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 6 \times 10^{24} \times 500}{2 \times 10^7} = 10^{10} \text{ J} $$

Answer: \(10^{10} \text{ J}\)

Q5: What is the binding energy of the satellite described in Q4?

Total Energy \( E = -K = -10^{10} \text{ J} \) Binding Energy = \( -E \)

Answer: \(10^{10} \text{ J}\)

Q6: What is the approximate height of a geostationary satellite above the equator?

Using the relation \( h = \left( \frac{T^2 R^2 g}{4\pi^2} \right)^{1/3} - R \) and substituting \( T = 24 \text{ hrs} = 86400 \text{ s} \), we get \( h \approx 3.6 \times 10^7 \text{ m} \).

Answer: 36,000 km
